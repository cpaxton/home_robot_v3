\section{PandaOmron mobile-manipulation harness and grasp-retention qualification in RoboCasa}
\label{app:robocasa_wbc_pick_place}

\paragraph{Contract and provenance boundary.}
To evaluate whole-body mobile manipulation in household kitchen scenes~\cite{robocasa2024,nasiriany2026robocasa365}
above the embodiment-agnostic control contract of Appendix~\ref{app:whole_body_control},
we bind \texttt{RoboCasaPandaBackend} directly to the composed \texttt{PandaOmron}
MuJoCo plant (planar three-DoF Omron LD-60 base, one-DoF prismatic torso,
seven-DoF torque-controlled Panda arm, and position-controlled parallel-jaw
gripper) at $20\,\text{Hz}$ control ($500\,\text{Hz}$ physics).
The actor interface remains strictly sensor-only (reset-anchored base pose and
body twist, joint positions/velocities/torques, end-effector pose and target,
gripper encoders, and measured robot contact state); simulator body identifiers
and object poses never enter the actor observation or checkpoint.
In both the teacher and learned-controller runs reported here, task-level grasp
and receptacle targets are derived from privileged simulator geometry and
explicitly tagged \texttt{privileged\_gt\_metadata}. All robot and object motion
executes in continuous native MuJoCo contact physics with zero object
attachments/welds and zero loaded \texttt{qpos} teleportation.

\paragraph{Holonomic base kinematics, stiction compensation, and carried transport.}
Inspecting the composed \texttt{omron\_mobile\_base.xml} plant exposed two
actuation mismatches that previously stalled non-teleported base driving and
ejected carried free-joint objects:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pre-yaw slide axes and hinge lever arm.}
  The planar slides (\texttt{joint\_mobile\_forward}, \texttt{joint\_mobile\_side})
  precede \texttt{joint\_mobile\_yaw} in the kinematic chain, and the yaw hinge
  is anchored at $(-0.21, 0, 0)\,\text{m}$ in the chassis frame. Consequently,
  the slide joints translate in the episode spawn frame rather than the rotated
  chassis heading. Given chassis forward/left unit vectors
  $(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_b, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_b)$, world slide axes
  $(\mathbf{a}_0, \mathbf{a}_1)$, and hinge-to-chassis lever vector
  $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p}_b - \mathbf{p}_{\text{yaw}}$, desired chassis twist
  $(v_x, v_y, \omega_z)$ maps to joint rates via
  $\mathbf{v}_{\text{slide}} = v_x \hat{\mathbf{x}}_b + v_y \hat{\mathbf{y}}_b - \omega_z (-r_y, r_x)^\top$
  and $\dot{q}_i^{\text{des}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{slide}}^\top \mathbf{a}_i$
  ($i \in \{0, 1\}$), with the exact inverse applied in \texttt{\_observation()}.
  \item \textbf{Coulomb frictionloss deadzones.}
  All three planar joints specify $F_{\text{fric}} = 250\,\text{N}$ dry friction
  against velocity servos with $k_v \in \{1000, 1000, 1500\}$, creating an
  uncompensated deadzone $|u| \le F_{\text{fric}} / k_v = 0.25\,\text{m/s}$
  ($0.167\,\text{rad/s}$ in yaw); similarly, \texttt{joint\_torso\_height}
  specifies $1000\,\text{N}$ frictionloss. Adding smooth Coulomb feedforward
  $u_i = \dot{q}_i^{\text{des}} + (F_{\text{fric},i} / k_{v,i})\tanh(\dot{q}_i^{\text{des}} / \epsilon)$
  with $1.0\,\text{m/s}^2$ slew-rate limiting enables millimeter-accurate dynamic
  gliding at low transport speeds and full $[0.02, 0.34]\,\text{m}$ torso travel.
  \item \textbf{Continuous physics transport vs.\ discrete \texttt{qpos} stepping.}
  Kinematically stepping planar \texttt{qpos} at $20\,\text{Hz}$ while holding a
  free-joint object teleports the $8\,\text{mm}$-thick finger pads $5\text{--}15\,\text{mm}$
  in zero simulation time, opening an air gap on one pad while burying the
  opposing pad into the mesh ($>180\,\text{N}$ solver impulse). Driving the base
  dynamically through \texttt{sim.step()} ($<0.45\,\text{mm}$ per $2\,\text{ms}$
  substep) while locking the retracted arm target in the moving chassis frame
  (\texttt{hold\_ee\_in\_base\_frame}) eliminates transport slip. After each base
  phase, \texttt{reanchor\_origin()} re-centers \texttt{base\_pose} at $(0,0,0)$
  while preserving world end-effector targets.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Grasp-frame geometry, edge-sliced narrow-axis planning, and retention gates.}
Task-space grasp frames target \texttt{gripper0\_right\_eef}, whereas the WBC
contract commands \texttt{robot0\_right\_hand}; the fixed rigid transform is
${}^{\text{hand}}\mathbf{T}_{\text{eef}} = (\mathbf{R}_z(-\pi/2), (0, 0, 0.097\,\text{m})^\top)$.
In \texttt{panda\_gripper.xml}, finger separation in \texttt{gripper0\_right\_eef}
lies along the EEF $X$-axis ($[\cos\psi, \sin\psi, 0]^\top$ under top-down yaw
$\psi$), with $79.0\,\text{mm}$ clear inner-pad opening at $q = [0.04, -0.04]$,
palm collision $31.0\,\text{mm}$ above EEF, and fingertip collision $9.2\,\text{mm}$
below EEF. World-fixed grasp yaw pins wrist \texttt{joint7} at its $+2.897\,\text{rad}$
limit; sweeping $\psi \in [\psi_{\text{base}}, \psi_{\text{base}} + \pi]$ centers
wrist posture around \texttt{\_READY\_Q[6]} ($+0.785\,\text{rad}$, $\ge 0.54\,\text{rad}$
joint margin) while covering all undirected 2D pinch directions.
To handle sparse two-ring cylinder meshes (cans) and eight-vertex boxes (soap
bars), the planner slices collidable triangle and primitive edges across five
planes spanning the $16\,\text{mm}$ rubber pad band, enforces counter and palm
clearance bounds $z_{\text{eef}} \ge \max(z_{\min} + 14.2\,\text{mm}, z_{\max} - 26.0\,\text{mm})$,
prefers the deepest safe height closest to CoM, and selects $\psi$ minimizing
projected pinch width along EEF $X$. Execution requires debounced bilateral
target contact ($>1\,\text{N}$ per finger for $0.9\,\text{s}$), $\ge 1\,\text{cm}$
measured object rise over a $3\,\text{cm}$ verification lift, post-transport
bilateral force verification, and RoboCasa's official \texttt{\_check\_success()}
(object inside fixture interior sites and gripper retracted $>0.25\,\text{m}$).
For upper wall cabinets (\texttt{PickPlaceCounterToCabinet}), the teacher lowers
the torso to $0.04\text{--}0.14\,\text{m}$ with a height-adaptive aisle-lean pitch to
grasp beneath the open cabinet door, retracts into the aisle, elevates the torso
to $0.34\,\text{m}$, and slides onto the $1.42\,\text{m}$ shelf at $55^\circ$
forward pitch.

\paragraph{Qualification summary and zero-shot policy diagnostic.}
Table~\ref{tab:robocasa_pandaomron_pick_place} summarizes the eleven teacher
qualification episodes archived in
\texttt{paper/data/whole\_body\_control/robocasa\_pandaomron\_pick\_place\_20261127.json}:
7/7 pass on \texttt{PickPlaceCounterToSink} and 4/4 pass on
\texttt{PickPlaceCounterToCabinet} (11/11 overall), retaining $13.2\text{--}20.1\,\text{N}$
per-finger normal load at the end of dynamic mobile-base transport, with up to
$1.04\,\text{m}$ of start-to-final object displacement and placing within $0.08\text{--}7.63\,\text{cm}$ XY of the target
interior site.
Evaluating the direct-plant reach checkpoint (\texttt{pandaomron\_robust})
zero-shot through the identical harness transfers dynamic base navigation directly
to the kitchen scene ($1.75\,\text{cm}$ XY arrival error in 237 steps) and holds
the base stationary during arm commands ($[+0.5, -1.1]\,\text{cm}$ drift) once
\texttt{reanchor\_origin()} is applied, but stops at \texttt{close\_gate} due to
a $0.296\,\text{m}$ / $2.19\,\text{rad}$ pregrasp residual: its direct-plant
training distribution sampled only $\pm 0.15\,\text{rad}$ joint offsets around
\texttt{\_READY\_Q} rather than $90^\circ$ top-down grasp postures.
The qualified teacher now exports paired $(\text{observation}, \text{teacher action})$
rollouts (\texttt{--save-rollout}) to close this task-posture distribution gap via
BC/DAgger, while observed RGB-D point-cloud target estimation remains a separate
gate before claiming unassisted benchmark task success.
